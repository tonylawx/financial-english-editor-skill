# Financial English Editor

## Overview

Turn Chinese financial writing into natural, publication-ready English for market readers. Do not translate sentence by sentence. Preserve the investment thesis, facts, numbers, tickers, levels, catalysts, risks, and positioning, then rewrite the piece in idiomatic financial English.

Core principle: **translate the market meaning, not the Chinese wording.**

## When to Use

Use this skill for:
- US stock, ETF, options, macro, earnings, sector, and technical-analysis commentary written in Chinese
- Daily market notes, newsletters, investment blogs, trading journals, research summaries, and social posts
- English drafts that are technically correct but sound translated, robotic, or unlike native financial writing
- Chinese trading idioms, metaphors, and shorthand that do not map naturally into English

Do not use this skill for legal translations, audited financial statements, or documents where literal wording is contractually required.

## Language Reference

For Chinese market jargon, trading idioms, options terminology, macro language, technical analysis, valuation language, positioning, flows, and tone mapping, consult `references/market-language.md`.

Do not treat the reference as a one-to-one dictionary. Pick the expression that matches the actual market meaning and rewrite the sentence when no natural direct equivalent exists.

## Workflow

### 1. Extract the market meaning first

Before rewriting, identify:
- **Thesis:** What is the author actually arguing?
- **Evidence:** What facts, price action, earnings data, macro data, or valuation points support it?
- **Catalysts:** What could move the asset next?
- **Risks:** What would invalidate or weaken the thesis?
- **Levels:** Preserve all prices, ranges, percentages, dates, yields, probabilities, and technical levels exactly unless they are clearly erroneous.
- **Positioning:** Is the author suggesting buy, trim, hold, hedge, wait, reload, avoid chasing, or watch for confirmation?

Do not start from Chinese syntax. Reconstruct the paragraph from these ideas.

### 2. Rewrite for a North American financial audience

Prefer language commonly seen in market commentary, research notes, and financial newsletters.

Use concise, concrete verbs:
- rise, gain, advance, rally, rebound
- fall, slide, drop, sell off, pull back
- break above, clear resistance, reclaim, hold support
- fade, stall, consolidate, coil, digest gains
- price in, reprice, compress multiples, expand margins
- trim, add, build a position, reload, hedge, stay sidelined

Prefer market-native phrasing over literal translation.

### 3. Preserve conviction accurately

Do not strengthen or weaken the author's view without evidence.

Examples:
- “可能” → may / could / appears to / likely, depending on context
- “大概率” → likely / more likely than not
- “确认” → confirmation only when the source really claims confirmation
- “看多” → bullish / constructive / positive on
- “不赌突破” → wait for confirmation / do not front-run the breakout

Avoid converting a tentative Chinese statement into a confident English claim.

### 4. Remove Chinglish and literal metaphors

Translate idioms by function.

Examples:

| Chinese | Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| 倒车接人 | pull back to pick up passengers | pullback offers a better entry / pullback looks buyable |
| 总开关 | master switch | key signal / main trigger / gating level |
| 不赌突破 | do not bet on the breakout | wait for a confirmed breakout / do not front-run the breakout |
| 高位磨 | grind at a high level | consolidate near the highs |
| 套牢盘 | trapped holders | overhead supply / sellers from prior highs |
| 抄底 | copy the bottom | buy the dip / bottom-fish, depending on tone |
| 接仓 | receive the position | take assignment / accept assignment |
| 减仓避险 | reduce position to avoid risk | trim exposure / de-risk |
| 放量突破 | break with enlarged volume | break out on strong volume / clear resistance with volume |
| 缩量横盘 | sideways with shrinking volume | consolidate on light volume |
| 估值被打 | valuation gets hit | multiples compress / valuation comes under pressure |
| 情绪修复 | sentiment repairs | sentiment improves / risk appetite recovers |

When no clean equivalent exists, rewrite the sentence instead of forcing a metaphor.

## Style Guide

### Voice

Aim for the middle ground between:
- Bloomberg-style market commentary
- sell-side research notes
- a sharp independent investor newsletter

The writing should sound informed and direct, not academic and not promotional.

### Sentence style

- Prefer short to medium sentences.
- One main market idea per sentence.
- Put the ticker or asset near the front when it is the subject.
- Use active voice where natural.
- Avoid excessive em dashes, rhetorical flourishes, and repetitive transitions.
- Avoid generic AI phrases such as “it is worth noting,” “this suggests that,” or “in the current market environment” unless genuinely useful.

### Tickers and numbers

- Keep ticker symbols uppercase: NVDA, GOOG, SPX, NDX, IGV.
- Preserve exact price levels and ranges.
- Use standard English financial formatting: $100, 3.6%, 10Y Treasury yield, Q2, EPS, CapEx.
- Do not silently change a figure because it looks unusual. Flag suspected errors separately.

### Titles

Do not translate titles literally. Rewrite for clarity and clickability while preserving the thesis.

Bad:
- “GOOG Pulls Back to Pick Up Passengers; Software Stuck at 107”

Better:
- “GOOG Pullback Looks Buyable; IGV Still Needs 107”
- “GOOG Offers a Better Entry as Software Tests Key Resistance”

Avoid sensationalism unless the Chinese source is deliberately sensational.

## Asset-Specific Language

### Stocks and ETFs

Prefer:
- upside/downside
- support/resistance
- breakout/breakdown
- relative strength/weakness
- multiple expansion/compression
- earnings revisions
- guidance
- positioning
- risk/reward
- catalyst
- valuation

### Options

Use standard derivatives terminology:
- sell/write a put or call
- short put / short call
- covered call
- cash-secured put
- credit spread / debit spread
- assignment / exercise
- expiration / DTE
- implied volatility / IV
- delta / gamma / theta / vega
- premium collected / premium paid
- roll out / roll up / roll down
- close for a profit / take profit

Never translate “接仓” as “receive shares”; use “take assignment” or “be assigned shares” when that is the meaning.

### Macro

Prefer:
- rate-cut expectations / hike expectations
- policy path
- inflation print
- labor-market cooling
- long-end yields
- financial conditions
- risk appetite
- growth expectations
- earnings sensitivity
- valuation sensitivity

### Technical analysis

Prefer:
- reclaim
- clear
- hold
- lose support
- break above / below
- consolidate
- test / retest
- gap
- supply zone / demand zone
- momentum
- volume confirmation

Avoid overclaiming precision. Technical levels are signals, not certainties.

## Two-Pass Editing

### Pass 1 — Fidelity

Check that the English preserves:
- thesis
- chronology
- causality
- caveats
- numbers
- tickers
- technical levels
- catalysts
- risks
- trade posture

### Pass 2 — Native-market rewrite

Ignore the Chinese sentence structure and ask:

> Would an experienced English-speaking investor or market writer naturally phrase it this way?

If not, rewrite it.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing, verify all of the following:

- No literal Chinese metaphors remain unless they are intentionally explained.
- No ticker, number, price level, date, or percentage was lost.
- No speculative statement became a fact.
- No causal relationship was invented.
- Titles sound natural in English.
- Verbs match actual market behavior.
- Options terminology is standard.
- Technical-analysis wording is conventional.
- The article can be read without knowing Chinese.
- The prose does not sound like a translation.

## Output Modes

When the user does not specify a format, return only the polished English version.

If useful, support these explicit modes:

- **faithful** — maximum fidelity, light stylistic editing
- **native** — default; preserve meaning while fully rewriting for natural financial English
- **newsletter** — sharper pacing and stronger headings for an investor newsletter
- **research-note** — more neutral, institutional tone
- **social** — concise X/Twitter-style financial English

## Example

Chinese source:

> GOOG今天大跌但没有明显利空，技术结构也没坏，更像倒车接人。软件这边IGV 107还是总开关，没突破之前不要赌突破。

Literal translation to avoid:

> GOOG fell sharply today without obvious bad news. The technical structure is not broken and it looks more like reversing to pick up passengers. IGV 107 is still the master switch for software. Do not bet on the breakout before it breaks through.

Preferred rewrite:

> GOOG sold off sharply despite no clear company-specific negative, while the broader technical structure remains intact. For now, the pullback looks more like a better entry than a trend break. In software, IGV still needs a clean move above 107 before the group turns decisively constructive, so it makes sense to wait for confirmation rather than front-run the breakout.
