## 11. Preferred Market-Writing Verbs

Use precise verbs instead of vague phrases.

- **Price:** rally, rebound, advance, climb, gain, fade, slip, slide, drop, tumble, plunge
- **Levels:** hold, defend, reclaim, clear, test, retest, reject, lose, break above, break below
- **Trend:** consolidate, coil, base, stall, accelerate, reverse, extend, digest
- **Valuation:** rerate, derate, reprice, compress, expand
- **Earnings:** beat, miss, guide, raise, cut, revise, deliver
- **Positioning:** add, trim, initiate, exit, scale in, scale out, hedge, de-risk, reload
- **Macro:** price in, reprice, tighten, ease, cool, reaccelerate, steepen, invert
- **Options:** write, exercise, assign, roll, hedge, decay, expire

## 12. Phrases to Avoid Unless Truly Natural

Avoid literal or AI-sounding constructions such as:

- “the market gave face”
- “pick up passengers” for 倒车接人
- “the master switch” for 总开关
- “repair sentiment”
- “enlarge volume”
- “receive the shares” for 接仓
- “digest pressure”
- “valuation is being beaten”
- “the logic is unchanged” when “the thesis remains intact” is better
- “do not gamble on the breakout” when “wait for confirmation” is better
- “high position” for 高位
- “low position” for 低位
- “capital side” for 资金面
- “policy side” for 政策面
- “fundamental side” for 基本面
- “good news / bad news” when catalyst, guidance, earnings, or policy language is more precise

## Final Rule

When a phrase has no clean one-to-one equivalent, **rewrite the sentence around the underlying market meaning**. Financial English should sound as if it was originally written in English, not translated from Chinese.
